# Lens: comments, documentation and naming

Comments, docstrings and names are among this repository's strengths. Every service, script and hook module opens with a docstring that states its purpose and an invariant. The inline comments in the largest modules of each language explain why rather than restate code. Ruff finds no commented-out code, owned source carries no TODO markers, units sit in names (micro\_usd, seconds, bytes), and each language follows one file-naming convention. The traceability practice is accurate: all 220 decision, 338 story and 364 requirement citations in source resolve, and nearly every one states the rule it cites. The weaknesses are drift and the absence of written, checked conventions. Nine comments in four languages describe an earlier state of the code: a 'later story', a 'first' tool set, a side panel that is now a modal, an app with 'no dialogs'. The root README and the developer Q&A still describe a product before implementation, with two models and Terra for every role. Names such as run\_intent, default\_registry, test\_final\_review and review\_majors record the story or review that introduced them rather than what they do, and five words (operation, profile, settings, adapters, workspace) each carry two or three meanings. None of the conventions the code follows is written down or checked: 36 'PRD x.y' citations name none of the three PRDs that number their sections that way, ERA and pep8-naming would pass today but are not selected, and nothing resolves citations or flags delivery wording. Contract documentation is thinnest on the largest entry points: 21 of 80 route handlers have a docstring, and the biggest screen components have no header. The area reviews cover single-file issues; these findings are the patterns shared across areas. [Back to the review](../README.md)
